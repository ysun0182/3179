1. Where are Melbourne's most expensive suburbs?

Median House and Unit Prices by Suburb

House prices are highest in inner Melbourne and parts of the Mornington Peninsula, with Portsea reaching $3.8 million in Q4 2025. In contrast, many outer suburbs have relatively lower house prices. Unit prices show a different spatial pattern, with higher prices concentrated in parts of inner and eastern Melbourne. Several outer suburbs also have no recorded quarterly unit median, rather than a price of zero.

Most Expensive House and Unit Suburbs

The most expensive house suburbs have exceptionally high median prices, with several locations standing well above the broader Melbourne market. The ranking highlights how premium house prices are concentrated in a relatively small number of suburbs.

The unit price ranking contains a different mix of suburbs compared with the house ranking. This suggests that expensive houses do not necessarily indicate equally expensive units, reflecting differences between the two property markets.

House vs Unit Prices in the Most Expensive Suburbs

Houses are consistently more expensive than units among the suburbs included in this comparison. However, the size of the price gap varies considerably between locations. Some expensive house suburbs show much larger differences, suggesting that house and unit prices do not always follow the same pattern within a suburb.

2. Where is the housing market most active?

House Price and Sales Activity by Suburb

The highest house sales volumes are concentrated in several moderately priced suburbs, particularly in Melbourne's outer areas. In contrast, many expensive inner suburbs record relatively fewer transactions. This indicates that suburbs with the highest property prices are not necessarily the most active housing markets.

House Sales across Different Price Levels

Price groups: Low (below $1.1 million), Medium ($1.1 million to $2.0 million), and High (above $2.0 million). Each point represents one suburb.

Lower-priced suburbs show both the highest sales volumes and the widest variation in market activity. Medium-priced suburbs generally have fewer sales, while the high-price group is concentrated at lower transaction levels. This reinforces the finding that more affordable housing markets often experience greater sales activity.

Is Market Activity Associated with Price Growth?

The nearly flat regression line suggests little clear linear relationship between house sales activity and annual price growth. However, suburbs with fewer transactions show a much wider range of price changes, including both increases and decreases. Higher-sales suburbs tend to be concentrated within a narrower range of annual price movements.

3. Does Public Transport Accessibility Relate to Housing Prices?

Public Transport Stops Across Melbourne

Public transport stops are distributed unevenly across Melbourne. Train stations follow major railway corridors extending from the inner city towards outer suburbs, while tram stops are concentrated in central Melbourne. Bus stops provide the widest geographical coverage, including many outer suburban areas with fewer train or tram stops.

Public Transport Stop Density and House Prices

Stop density = train, tram and bus stops per km². Low: fewer than 5 stops/km²; Medium: 5 to less than 11 stops/km²; High: 11 or more stops/km². CBD distance is measured in a straight line from each suburb's centroid.

House prices generally decrease as suburbs become further from Melbourne's CBD. Within several distance groups, suburbs with higher public transport stop density also tend to have higher prices. However, the low-density group within 10 km has the highest median price at $2.26 million, based on only three suburbs. This shows that the relationship is not consistent across all groups.

House Prices by Public Transport Stop Count

Suburbs are grouped by their total number of public transport stops: 35 stops or fewer and more than 35 stops.

Suburbs with more than 35 public transport stops have a slightly higher median house price ($1.10 million) than those with 35 stops or fewer ($1.02 million). However, the two groups show substantial overlap in their price distributions.

This suggests that stop count alone cannot explain house price differences. Larger suburbs may naturally contain more stops, while service frequency and actual accessibility are not considered.

Looking Back: Melbourne's Housing Price Trends

House and Unit Prices, 2015–2025

Each yearly value is the median of suburb-level median sale prices for the same 307 Melbourne suburbs. This is not the official transaction-weighted Melbourne median.

Both house and unit prices increased between 2015 and 2025, but houses experienced stronger growth. House prices rose by approximately 54.8%, compared with 34.7% for units. The most noticeable house price increase occurred around 2021, followed by relatively smaller changes in the following years.

Annual House Price Changes

The largest annual increase occurred in 2021, when the house price measure rose by $210,000. Although prices declined in several years, including 2019 and 2022, these decreases were smaller than the major increases. Overall, the value rose from $745,000 in 2015 to approximately $1.15 million in 2025.

The Bigger Picture: Price, Activity and Accessibility

Melbourne's most expensive suburbs are not necessarily its most active markets. While proximity to the CBD and public transport may relate to house prices, neither fully explains the differences between suburbs. Over the past decade, house prices have also grown faster than unit prices.
