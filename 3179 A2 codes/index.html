Melbourne Housing Market

This visualisation explores how residential property prices vary across Melbourne suburbs and how price relates to location, dwelling type, property characteristics, and accessibility.

1. Where are Melbourne's most expensive suburbs?

Median House and Unit Prices by Suburb

This choropleth map will compare median property prices across Melbourne suburbs.

Median property prices show a strong spatial pattern across Melbourne. House prices are generally higher in inner Melbourne, particularly in suburbs close to the CBD, while most outer suburbs have lower median prices. A second high-price cluster can be observed on the Mornington Peninsula, with Portsea recording a median house price of $3.8 million in Q4 2025. Unit prices show a different pattern. Higher unit prices are concentrated in parts of inner and eastern Melbourne, while many outer suburbs have no recorded unit median price for Q4 2025. These missing values do not represent a price of zero, but indicate that no usable median unit price was available for the quarter, reflecting the much lower presence or transaction activity of units in many outer suburban areas.

Most Expensive House and Unit Suburbs

These lollipop charts show the ten suburbs with the highest median house and unit prices in Q4 2025.

House vs Unit Prices in the Most Expensive Suburbs

This connected dot plot compares median house and unit prices among Melbourne's ten most expensive suburbs.

The two lollipop charts show that the suburbs with the highest house prices are not necessarily the same as those with the highest unit prices. The house ranking includes several extremely expensive suburbs, while the unit ranking contains a different mix of suburbs, suggesting that the two property types have distinct spatial price patterns.

The connected dot plot provides a more direct comparison by considering only suburbs with median prices available for both houses and units. As a result, several suburbs appearing in the top house-price ranking disappear from this chart because no unit median price was available for Q4 2025, which may reflect limited unit availability or low unit transaction activity. Among the remaining suburbs, house prices are consistently much higher than unit prices, with particularly large gaps in some high-priced suburbs. Together, the charts show that a high house price does not necessarily imply an equally high unit price, and that house and unit markets can behave differently even within the same suburb.

2. Where is the housing market most active?

House Price and Sales Activity by Suburb

This proportional symbol map will compare house transaction volume and median house price across suburbs.

Higher house prices do not necessarily correspond to higher sales activity. Suburbs with the greatest number of house sales are generally concentrated in the moderate price range, while many of Melbourne's most expensive suburbs record relatively lower transaction volumes. This suggests that market activity is not simply driven by property price, with more moderately priced suburbs accounting for a larger share of house transactions.

House Sales across Different Price Levels

This jitter plot compares house sales activity across suburbs with low, medium, and high median house prices in Q4 2025. Each point represents one Melbourne suburb.

Low-priced suburbs are defined as those with a median house price below $1.1 million, medium-priced suburbs have a median house price between $1.1 million and $2.0 million, and high-priced suburbs have a median house price above $2.0 million.

Sales activity varies considerably across all three price levels. However, the highest transaction volumes are concentrated in the low-price group, which also shows the widest spread in house sales. Medium-priced suburbs generally record lower sales volumes, while high-priced suburbs are more strongly concentrated at the lower end of the sales range. This reinforces the pattern observed in the map: higher house prices do not necessarily correspond to greater market activity.
